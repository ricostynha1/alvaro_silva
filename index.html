Making formal proofs usable.

I'm Álvaro, a software-verification researcher in Porto. I build tools that make verification-aware languages like Dafny easier to work with. That means LLMs that suggest the missing assertion, debuggers that point to the line where a proof broke, and diagnostics that check whether a verified spec says anything useful. I'm advised by Alexandra Mendes (FEUP / INESC TEC) and Ruben Martins (Carnegie Mellon).

02 / Research

Four threads, one question

What would make a working developer reach for a verifier, and keep using it?

LLM-assisted proof

Language models that fill in the tedious parts of a proof, such as helper assertions and lemmas, while the verifier checks every suggestion. I also study how students use these assistants when they write proofs.

Debugging & repair

When verification fails, I want the tool to say where and why. This thread covers fault localization from counterexamples, single-transformation repair, and the FCT-funded VeriFixer project with INESC TEC, INESC-ID and CMU.

ISSRE '26DafnyFixVeriFixer

Specification quality

A proof that verifies can still rest on a weak spec. ProofPulse measures which spec elements a proof actually relies on, and DSpec2Test turns the spec itself into a test suite.

ProofPulseDSpec2TestMutDafny
rtl ∪ rust iv

Beyond Dafny

I spent three years verifying hardware with SystemVerilog, UVM and VCFormal, and I want to bring that experience back into the work. Current interests are Dafny as a language for hardware models, Verus for verified Rust, and agent-aware editors such as TraceLean.
